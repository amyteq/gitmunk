## Unreleased

- `dev-hooks` 0.2.4: at the guard levels `med` (the default) and `high`, plain data no longer draws a `guard_tool.py` ask (#81). When the hook would ask, it reads the command again and abstains if only plain data drew the ask, writing `dev-hooks: <level> level leaves plain-data text to Claude's permissions` to stderr. Plain data here means: a word in an operand of `cat`, `head`, `tail` or `grep` (`rm` in a file name or a quoted pattern, a quoted `git clean`), single-quoted Markdown backticks in `echo` or `printf` text and in a plain `sed s/…/…/` script, `awk` fields in a program that runs nothing, a program spelt with a literal `/` (`$S/tool.sh`), a loop variable set over literal words next to an unrelated `$(…)`, unless the shell also sets that name (`REPLY`, `_`, `OPTARG`), and a literal relative target of a direct `rm` right after a leading `cd $TMPDIR/… &&`. Operands and scripts count as data only in a line whose every command is a direct call of `cat`, `head`, `tail`, `grep`, `echo`, `printf`, `sed`, `awk` or `tee`, with no wrapper (`env`, `xargs`, `nice`), assignment prefix, program path, `.`, `source`, group or substitution, with no `$` or backtick outside single quotes and no comment, heredoc, glob, brace, `~` or `=` outside quotes, and no redirection to a `/dev/` path other than `/dev/null` (bash's `/dev/tcp/…` is a socket), and only when each such word is quoted or plain text the shell does not expand. Words that expand (a brace, `=name`, a glob or `~` in an `rm` target or a script), text another program may run (an interpreter in the line, a pipe into anything but a reader or printer, a `sed` `e` or `w` flag, a `sed` or `awk` option other than `-n`, `-E`, `-r`, `-i` or `-F`, or a non-literal word, anywhere around the script, `awk` `system` or `>`), and every deny keep their checks. At `low`, with `dangerouslyDisableSandbox` and on other platforms the decisions are unchanged. A replay of 533 command strings (the 0.2.3 corpus, the #81 shapes and near misses, and every string the 0.2.3 tests pass to the guard) through the 0.2.3 and 0.2.4 hooks changed no deny and no ask other than the #81 shapes at `med` and `high`. The shapes of candidates 1–4 and 6 and the first shape of candidate 7 no longer ask at `med` and `high`. Still asking: candidate 5, `cd $TMPDIR/run && cat > notes.txt <<'EOF'`, `It's ready.`, `EOF`, `node gen.mjs notes.txt`, at every level, because an interpreter in the same line may run what the heredoc wrote, and the hook cannot tell a body it runs (`bash run.sh`) from one it only reads; candidate 7's second shape, `CHK="$(date -u)"; cat $S/a.md | sed "s/@@CHECKED@@/$CHK/" > $S/b.md`, at every level when the read skip does not apply (where it applies, 0.2.3 already let it through), because `$CHK` carries the substitution's output into the `sed` script, where text such as `x/;e cmd;#` would run a command, so the hook cannot tell it from a substitution that injects one; candidate 8, `cd $TMPDIR/run && rm -f watch.state`, and candidate 9, `rm $TMPDIR/run/old.sh`, wherever the hook has no `TMPDIR` of its own, as in the measured sessions, because the hook cannot see the sandbox's `TMPDIR`, and without the sandbox an unset `TMPDIR` expands to nothing, so the path may lie outside any temp root. With the hook's `TMPDIR` set to a temp root, candidate 8 no longer asks and candidate 9 already did not.

- `dev-skills` 0.4.1: `dev-batch` gains two opt-in settings (#58). `slots` names resources next to the cap, such as `slots=heavy=3,e2e=1,live-api=1:implement+review`; without a stage list a slot covers every stage. A stage of a row whose issue has the label `slot:<name>` (any case) launches only while fewer than `<count>` running sessions hold that slot. The cap still applies, and a later row that needs no full slot launches meanwhile. A session holds the slots it was launched or adopted with, recorded on its `sessions.json` record, while it is `working`, `blocked`, `unknown` or `limited`, even if the label is removed. A row's own limited sessions free their slots for its replacement, as for the cap. `B launch` refuses `slot-full`, naming the slot and its holders. `cost_alert=<multiple>` queues one `cost-alert` judgment for each row whose recorded cost, the sum of its `costUsd` in `metrics.jsonl`, is greater than that multiple of the median recorded cost of the batch's rows. Each row is alerted at most once per batch, and the alert changes no status, Next or launch. With the defaults, `slots=none` and `cost_alert=off`, the batch behaves as before; an invalid value is refused with exit `2`.
- `dev-skills` 0.4.0: new `dev-reply` (#78). It finds the comments that mention the signed-in user in this repository's open issues, PRs and discussions, or in one thread or comment, and that the user has not answered yet: no later text of theirs in the same reply thread, and posted within `since=` (default `5d`). Texts the user wrote, quotes, code, links, emails and team mentions do not count. With several topics the owner picks one or all. For each topic it summarizes the background, then asks every question and request as `dev-interview` popups with a recommended option first, pushing back with evidence where a request is unreasonable, costly or risky. A request for work is asked as reply only, a small action after a confirm (a label, an assignee, closing or reopening the thread), a new issue that can also be added to the `dev-batch` batch on hold, or a typed answer. It then drafts one reply per sender in the sender's language, in the comment's own thread where GitHub has one (a discussion comment or a PR review thread), else as a comment that links what it answers. A person is mentioned with `@login`; a bot or an `@claude`/`@codex` trigger stays in a code span. `cc=` adds a copy per destination in that thread's language, in this repository or in another private one. Every reply, copy and action waits for the owner's confirm, and `dry-run` only lists the topics. The helper `check-reply.mjs` resolves the invocation and finds the unanswered mentions; its tests cover both. `dev-skills` has eleven skills; the translations in `i18n/` follow in their own update.

## 0.6.0

- `dev-skills` 0.3.4: `dev-refine-issue`'s `/goal` completion condition names only the acceptance criteria shown offline at the PR head (#57). For a task delivered as a PR, each criterion now ends its first line with `Shown at: PR head.`, `Shown at: review.` or `Shown at: owner.`. A criterion shown by the review or by the owner is listed after `Handed on, not goal conditions:` with its stage, because the implement session cannot meet it. A named owner gate or another blocker ends the goal once its question is posted on the issue, and the session then ends without waiting; a cancellation or budget stop ends it without a question. The plain-language prompt is the `/goal` line without `/goal `. The `/goal` line stays one line in the same place, so `dev-batch` reads and launches it as before. Tasks not delivered as a PR keep the earlier completion condition, and task comments already published are not rewritten. Two fixtures and their tests cover both cases.
- `dev-hooks` 0.2.3 adds scoped guard levels for quoted Markdown assembly and session scratchpad files (#66). The default `med` handles a single named scratch file. The optional `high` handles multiple files and a fixed-string search with a file-supplied needle. Each time the hook leaves such a command to Claude's permissions, it writes one stderr line naming the level and the case. See [Guard levels](docs/install.md#guard-levels).
- `dev-skills` 0.3.3: `dev-review-pr` records each phase's token usage (#46). Every phase record has a `usage` object (`input`, `cached_input`, `cache_write`, `output`, `reasoning` when reported, `cost_usd`, `source`), or `usage: null` with a `usage_reason` of `not-run`, `not-reported` or `malformed`; usage never changes a phase's status. A Claude phase sums its JSON result's `modelUsage` and takes `total_cost_usd`. A Codex phase now runs `codex exec --json -o <file>`, still `--ephemeral`: the helper sums its `turn.completed` events as they arrive, takes the session ID from its single `thread.started` event, and publishes the `-o` last message plus one newline, the bytes the report had before. Under `--json` Codex reports no model, provider or effort, so a Codex phase records the values the helper passed, marked ` (requested)`, with the provider `openai (default)` when unset, and a Codex phase that pins no model is refused (`codex-model`). Reviewer and writer signatures gain a `USAGE` field such as `in 1.24M (cached 1.10M) · out 18k`, omitted when usage is unknown. The report marker and the `Base:`, `Reviewed head:` and `Verdict:` lines are unchanged, so `dev-merge` and `dev-batch` read reports as before.
- `dev-skills` 0.3.2 adds `check-text` for checking batches of literal quotations in local evidence snapshots; a quotation that is missing or on more than one line fails the batch. Reviewer and writer handoffs carry candidate quotations back to the coordinator, who checks their line numbers before publication.
- Settings templates (#67): `templates/claude/settings.json` (stable) and `settings.beta.json` (beta) carry a portable personal development profile: grouped command permissions with no shell allow rule, sandbox settings with auto-approval off, project-neutral classifier instructions, and model, retention, UI and Remote Control preferences. Each selects its channel's marketplace and only the gitmunk plugins. `docs/install.md` names the two plugin-registration fields to share with a team and treats the rest as opt-in at user scope. No plugin changes.
- `dev-hooks` 0.2.2: fewer `guard_tool.py` prompts (#61). In a 48-hour measurement, 223 of 299 prompts were hook asks, and every one was approved. The substitution rule now reads one simple command at a time: a `$(…)`, backtick, `<(…)` or variable asks only in a command whose program is `rm`, `git push`, a transfer or a read program, and single-quoted text and quoted heredoc bodies are data unless another program may run them as commands. A variable assigned a literal earlier in the same command resolves, so `S=$TMPDIR/run; rm -rf $S/old` and `cat $S/a.log` no longer ask, and a protected path reached through a variable is now denied. When the settings files show that the call runs sandboxed, with `failIfUnavailable` on and reads of the 22 protected home and project paths denied and not re-opened by a read or write path, read programs also skip the substitution ask; any missing, unreadable or doubtful setting keeps today's asks. A command that changes `IFS` no longer counts `$TMPDIR` as a temp root. The install guide's new [Fewer prompts](docs/install.md#fewer-prompts) section gives the measured categories, the settings for the read skip, and how to drop user-level hooks that point at project files and narrow broad `ask` rules.
- `dev-skills` 0.3.1: `dev-review-pr` Codex phases work with Codex's standalone installer (#23). That installer puts `codex` inside its login directory (`~/.codex/packages/standalone/releases/<release>/bin/codex`), where the bounded worker mounted nothing, so every command failed and the phase was still recorded `completed`. When the first `codex` on `PATH` resolves to exactly that layout, the helper now mounts that one release directory read-only and starts every Codex call with the resolved binary. The rest of the login directory, its credentials, sessions and configuration included, stays unmounted. Before every Codex phase the helper also runs `codex sandbox` with `true` under the phase's permission profile, and refuses the phase with a `bounded-tools` blocker naming the cause unless it exits 0. A probe timeout ends the phase as `timeout`. A `codex` anywhere else inside the login directory is refused there, and the helper never falls back to Codex's built-in read-only sandbox.
- `dev-batch` skill (#44, #45): runs a batch of issues through refine, implement, review and squash merge as background sessions across accounts, with one ledger in the source issue or discussion and merges only under the owner's authorization line. A free slot goes to the issue with the highest priority label (`P0`, `priority:high` and similar; none ranks as P2), then the lowest Batch, then row order. `dev-skills` 0.3.0 (ten skills). The translations in `i18n/` follow in their own update.
- `dev-skills` 0.2.4: `dev-track` links each processed discussion back to its tracker (#48). Under the invisible done mark, `H body` adds one visible line, `> 📋 Registered in tracker [#N · <tracker title>](<tracker URL>)`, or `已登记到台账` for a Chinese discussion, in both the default and `meetings` playbooks. A URL other than `https://<host>/<owner>/<repo>/discussions/N` is refused, and `[`, `]` and `\` in the title are escaped. Discussions processed earlier keep their bodies, because a `redo` still never rewrites one.
- `dev-skills` 0.2.3: `dev-track`'s `meetings` digest gives every TODO row a permanent ID, `D<discussion>-T<k>` (#47). The `ID` column comes first in the English and the Chinese TODO table, also with one row, and `k` counts from 1 in row order. ## 0.5.1

- `dev-skills` 0.2.2 (released from 0.2.2-beta.1): new `dev-interview` (#50). It collects the owner's pending decisions (`type=OD|SD|ALL`) on one issue, PR or discussion, and with `linked` on its open sub-issues and the threads its body links, then asks them in rounds of up to four popups. The recommended option comes first. After each round, before the next, it records every answer where its item sits: the item's status mark and a row in one decisions block of that body or comment, or a reply when the place cannot be edited. Free-text answers are kept verbatim, partial answers stay partly open, and requested work is listed as a follow-up, never done. `DEV_INTERVIEW_ASK_LANG` sets the popup language (default: the session's language) and `DEV_INTERVIEW_NOTE_LANG` the language written back (default: each source's language); both fall back to `zh-CN`. `dry-run` prints the plan only.
- `dev-merge`'s guarded `edit` also writes discussion bodies and comments, and `dev-explain`'s argument parsing is shared with `dev-interview`. Neither skill's behaviour changes.
